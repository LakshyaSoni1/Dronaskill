HOW TO USE THIS FILE
---------------------
This is the backup for the "What makes us different" slide and for
the Q&A that follows it. Section 4 is the table to put on a slide.
Sections 5-9 are what you SAY, not what you show.

Read Section 8 (where we don't win) before you present. Judges trust
a team that names its own gaps far more than one that claims to beat
everyone at everything. You will almost certainly be asked.

VERIFY BEFORE YOU PRESENT: this file deliberately compares BUSINESS
MODELS and STRUCTURAL INCENTIVES, not prices or feature checklists,
because those change every quarter. If you decide to quote a price
or a specific competitor feature on a slide, check it yourself that
week. Do not put a number on screen you cannot defend.

1. THE ONE-LINE ANSWER
====================================================================

  "Everyone else sells you content. We tell you what to learn,
   in what order, and whether you're actually ready."

If you only get one sentence, that is the sentence. Content is a
commodity now — there are ten free tutorials for every topic in our
catalogue. The scarce thing is SEQUENCE and FEEDBACK: what to learn
next, and proof you've learned it. That is the gap DronaSkill fills.

The second sentence, if you get one:

  "And nobody's incentive is pointed at keeping you on the app
   instead of getting you a job."

2. WHO WE ARE ACTUALLY COMPETING WITH
====================================================================

Be honest here — it wins you credibility. Our real competition is
NOT Coursera. It is, in order of how many students actually use it:

  1. YouTube + a browser tab      (the default, and it is free)
  2. ChatGPT / Claude directly    (fast-growing, genuinely good)
  3. Coding-practice sites        (LeetCode, GFG, HackerRank)
  4. Paid bootcamps               (Scaler, Masai, Newton-type)
  5. Course marketplaces          (Udemy, Coursera, Unacademy, PW)
  6. The college placement cell   (the incumbent nobody names)

Saying "our competitor is YouTube" out loud is a strong move. It
signals you understand your user's actual behaviour instead of
picking a flattering comparison.

3. THE STRUCTURAL ARGUMENT (the one that actually holds)
====================================================================

Every competitor's weakness comes from HOW THEY MAKE MONEY, not
from bad engineering. This is the argument judges remember, because
it cannot be fixed with a feature release.

  AD-FUNDED (YouTube)
    Revenue = time on platform. Every design decision -- autoplay,
    suggested videos, comments, the infinite sidebar -- exists to
    extend a session. A tool that got you to your answer and closed
    itself would destroy their revenue. They cannot build us.

  ENROLMENT-FUNDED (bootcamps)
    Revenue = seats sold, often at a very high ticket price. The
    incentive is to enrol everyone into the same cohort track,
    because personalised paths don't scale a fixed syllabus. Also
    optimised to make you feel you NEED the programme.

  CATALOGUE-FUNDED (Udemy, Coursera, Unacademy, PW)
    Revenue = courses sold or subscriptions. The incentive is a
    bigger catalogue, not a shorter path. More courses looks like
    more value; telling you "you can skip these four" does not.

  ENGAGEMENT-FUNDED (practice sites, streak apps)
    Revenue = daily actives and premium upsell. The incentive is
    streaks and problem counts, which measure ACTIVITY, not
    readiness.

  DRONASKILL
    No ads, no impressions, no seat sales, nothing to upsell inside
    the learning loop. There is no revenue reason to keep a student
    on the app one minute longer than the task needs. That is why we
    can build a player with no suggested videos, and why we can tell
    a student "tick this, you already know it, skip 40 hours."

  SAY THIS: "Ask yourself what each of these companies loses if you
  learn faster. For us, the answer is nothing."

5. HEAD-TO-HEAD: YOUTUBE
====================================================================

WHAT THEY DO WELL (say this first, always)
  Effectively infinite free content, often better taught than paid
  courses. Our own 214 curated lectures are YouTube videos. We are
  built ON it, not against it.

WHERE WE WIN
  - Sequence. YouTube gives you a video; we give you skill #7 of 41
    with the six before it already ticked off.
  - No rabbit hole. Our player strips suggested videos, comments and
    autoplay. Every one of those exists to end your study session.
  - Memory. YouTube forgets you the moment you close the tab. We
    keep the roadmap, the ticks, the logged hours and the charts.
  - Curation with a guarantee: every video is checked for
    embeddability before it enters the catalogue, so no dead
    "Video unavailable" mid-path.

HOW TO SAY IT
  "We didn't try to replace YouTube's content. We replaced the
   part of YouTube that's working against you."

6. HEAD-TO-HEAD: CHATGPT / CLAUDE USED DIRECTLY
====================================================================

Take this one seriously. It is the fastest-growing competitor and
the one a technical judge is most likely to raise.

WHAT THEY DO WELL
  Answers any doubt instantly, better than our offline tier, and
  students already have it open.

WHERE WE WIN
  - Context. A blank chatbot doesn't know you paused CS231n at
    14:20, scored 85% on gradients, and are four skills into an ML
    track. Guru AI sends that with every question.
  - The answer becomes an ASSET. In a chatbot, your doubt scrolls
    away forever. Here it becomes a revision card in tomorrow's
    plan. Learning is retention, not one-time explanation.
  - It refuses to bluff. When our offline tier has no match, it says
    so and offers to log an open doubt. A general chatbot will
    always produce a confident answer, right or wrong.
  - Voice-first, hands-free: ask out loud, hear the answer, keep
    your hands on the problem.

HONEST CAVEAT (do not hide this)
  Our LIVE tier needs the user's own Anthropic API key today,
  because we run fully client-side with no backend. Without a key it
  falls back to a 28-topic offline base. Say this plainly and pivot:
  "the architecture is done and proven; the hosted key is a backend
  problem, not a product problem."

HOW TO SAY IT
  "A chatbot answers your question. Guru AI answers your question
   and then puts it in tomorrow's revision plan."

7. HEAD-TO-HEAD: THE OTHER THREE
====================================================================

PAID BOOTCAMPS (Scaler / Masai / Newton-type)
  They win on: live human mentors, peer cohorts, accountability,
  placement networks, an actual brand on your CV. Concede all of it.
  We win on: cost (free), starting from what you ALREADY know
  instead of week 1 for everybody, and no lock-in to a fixed
  syllabus or an income-share agreement.
  SAY: "We're not trying to beat a mentor. We're what the 95% of
  students who will never pay for one actually get instead."

COURSE MARKETPLACES (Udemy / Coursera / Unacademy / PW)
  They win on: recognised certificates, instructor brands,
  production quality, breadth.
  We win on: a catalogue is not a plan. They sell you a course; we
  tell you which four of their courses you can skip. Also: their
  personalisation happens after you pay, ours happens before you
  sign up at all.
  SAY: "A course catalogue answers 'what exists.' We answer 'what
  next.'"

PRACTICE SITES (LeetCode / GFG / HackerRank)
  They win on: problem volume, contests, company-tagged questions.
  We win on: they measure problems solved, which is activity, not
  readiness. We measure logged hours against a real target, plus
  spoken interview answers with pace and filler-word feedback --
  which is what an actual interview tests and a code editor never
  does.
  SAY: "Solving 300 problems doesn't tell you if you can explain one
  of them out loud, under pressure, to a stranger."

8. WHERE WE DO NOT WIN (READ THIS BEFORE PRESENTING)
====================================================================

Volunteer two of these before you're asked. It buys enormous
credibility and it defuses the question.

  1. NO HUMAN MENTOR. A bootcamp's mentor beats our AI for
     motivation and accountability. We don't compete there.

  2. NO RECOGNISED CREDENTIAL. Our verification is self-contained;
     it isn't yet a certificate an employer knows. Roadmap item.

  3. DATA LIVES IN THE BROWSER. Clear your browser data and your
     progress is gone. No cross-device sync. This is a deliberate
     zero-backend tradeoff for shipping speed, not an oversight.

  4. MOCK INTERVIEW GRADING IS A HEURISTIC TODAY. Keyword coverage,
     answer length, filler words. The real LLM rubric is written and
     mapped to the UI, waiting on a backend.

  5. LIVE AI NEEDS THE USER'S OWN KEY. See Section 6.

  6. CONTENT DEPENDS ON YOUTUBE. If a video is pulled, that slot
     needs re-curation. Mitigated by pre-checking embeddability, not
     eliminated.

  SAY THIS: "Every one of these is a backend problem with a known
  solution, not a design flaw. We chose to prove the product loop
  first and pay for infrastructure second."

9. "WHY CAN'T A BIG PLAYER JUST COPY THIS?"
====================================================================

The single hardest question. The answer is NOT "we'll move faster."

  YOUTUBE CAN'T, because a distraction-free player deletes their
  revenue model. They would have to remove the sidebar, autoplay and
  comments -- the three things that generate their watch time.

  BOOTCAMPS CAN'T EASILY, because "here's a path that skips 40 hours
  you already know" directly shrinks the product they sell seats to.

  MARKETPLACES CAN'T EASILY, because their catalogue economics
  reward you buying MORE courses, and our whole value is telling you
  to take FEWER.

  THE HONEST PART: any of them COULD build it if they were willing
  to break their own business model. Historically, incumbents don't.
  That's the opening.

  SAY THIS: "It's not that they can't build it. It's that building
  it would cost them more than it earns them. That gap is our
  window."
